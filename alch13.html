Enzyme Inhibitors and Cofactors

Notes

1. Enzyme inhibitors

Enzyme inhibitors bind to enzymes and reduce their activity, slowing or stopping enzyme-catalysed reactions.

  • Reversible inhibitors: form weak interactions and can detach, allowing enzyme activity to recover.
  • Irreversible inhibitors: form strong bonds and permanently inactivate the affected enzyme molecules.

2. Competitive inhibition

  • Competitive inhibitors have a shape similar to the substrate and bind to the active site.
  • They compete with substrate molecules and reduce enzyme-substrate complex formation.
  • At a given moderate substrate concentration, the reaction rate is lower.
  • Increasing substrate concentration can overcome reversible competitive inhibition: substrate molecules are more likely to occupy active sites.
  • The same maximum rate can be reached at sufficiently high substrate concentration.

3. Non-competitive inhibition

  • Non-competitive inhibitors bind at a site separate from the active site, described as an allosteric site in the lesson.
  • Binding changes enzyme conformation and reduces its catalytic activity.
  • In the lesson's simplified model, the active site changes shape so the substrate no longer fits.
  • Adding more substrate cannot overcome the inhibition because the inhibitor does not compete for the active site.
  • The maximum reaction rate is lower. For reversible inhibition, activity can recover when the inhibitor detaches.

Clarification: non-competitive inhibition does not always prevent substrate binding; it can reduce catalysis even when substrate still binds. Reversibility and binding location describe different aspects of inhibition.

4. Metabolic pathways and end-product inhibition

A metabolic pathway is a series of reactions in which each product becomes the substrate for the next step.

Substrate 1 → Substrate 2 → Substrate 3 → End product

Enzymes 1, 2 and 3 catalyse the successive steps.

  • When the end product accumulates, it inhibits an enzyme earlier in the pathway, such as enzyme 1.
  • This reduces the supply of intermediates and slows subsequent reactions.
  • This negative feedback prevents overproduction and conserves energy and resources.
  • The inhibition is reversible: when end-product concentration falls, the pathway can resume.

5. Cofactors

Cofactors are non-protein substances that assist enzyme activity. They may be inorganic ions or organic molecules.

  • Chloride ions act as a cofactor for amylase.
  • Amylase breaks down starch into smaller sugars, such as maltose.

6. Coenzymes and prosthetic groups

  • Coenzymes: organic cofactors, often derived from vitamins. In the lesson, they bind temporarily to assist enzyme function.
  • Coenzyme A: made using vitamin B5; carries acyl groups, including acetyl groups, in pathways involved in respiration.
  • Prosthetic groups: tightly bound cofactors that remain attached during catalysis.
  • Zinc ions in carbonic anhydrase: the example of a tightly bound cofactor. This enzyme helps regulate blood pH by catalysing the interconversion of carbon dioxide and water with bicarbonate and hydrogen ions.

Cofactors include both inorganic ions and organic molecules. “Coenzyme” describes an organic cofactor, while “prosthetic group” describes tight attachment; these terms are not necessarily mutually exclusive.

Quiz: 40 Short Questions

Explain enzyme inhibition, end-product feedback, and the roles of cofactors, coenzymes and prosthetic groups.

Write an answer, then open “Show answer” to check your work.

1. What is an enzyme inhibitor?

Show answer

A molecule that binds to an enzyme and reduces its activity.

2. What effect can an inhibitor have on an enzyme-catalysed reaction?

Show answer

It can slow down or stop the reaction.

3. What type of bonds do reversible inhibitors form with enzymes?

Show answer

Weak bonds that allow the inhibitor to detach.

4. Why can enzyme activity recover after reversible inhibition?

Show answer

The inhibitor can detach, allowing the enzyme to function again.

5. How do irreversible inhibitors differ from reversible inhibitors?

Show answer

Irreversible inhibitors form strong bonds and permanently inactivate the affected enzyme; reversible inhibitors can detach.

6. What determines whether an inhibitor is competitive or non-competitive?

Show answer

Where it binds to the enzyme and whether it competes with the substrate for the active site.

7. Where does a competitive inhibitor bind?

Show answer

At the active site of the enzyme.

8. Why can a competitive inhibitor fit into the active site?

Show answer

It has a shape similar to that of the substrate.

9. How does a competitive inhibitor reduce enzyme-substrate complex formation?

Show answer

It occupies the active site, preventing the substrate from binding there.

10. How can the effect of a reversible competitive inhibitor be overcome?

Show answer

By increasing the substrate concentration sufficiently.

11. Why does a high substrate concentration reduce competitive inhibition?

Show answer

Substrate molecules are more likely to occupy active sites than inhibitor molecules.

12. How does a competitive inhibitor affect reaction rate at the same moderate substrate concentration?

Show answer

The reaction rate is lower than it is without the inhibitor.

13. Can the same maximum rate be reached with a reversible competitive inhibitor present?

Show answer

Yes, if the substrate concentration is sufficiently high.

14. Where does a non-competitive inhibitor bind?

Show answer

At a site separate from the active site, called an allosteric site in the lesson.

15. What is an allosteric site?

Show answer

A binding site on an enzyme that is separate from its active site.

16. How can non-competitive inhibitor binding affect the active site?

Show answer

It changes the enzyme's conformation, reducing the active site's ability to bind substrate or catalyse the reaction.

17. Why does increasing substrate concentration not overcome non-competitive inhibition?

Show answer

The inhibitor does not compete with the substrate for the active site, and adding substrate does not restore inhibited enzyme activity.

18. How does non-competitive inhibition affect the maximum reaction rate?

Show answer

It lowers the maximum reaction rate.

19. What must happen for an enzyme to recover from reversible non-competitive inhibition?

Show answer

The inhibitor must detach so the enzyme can return to its functional conformation.

20. A reaction reaches its original maximum rate after more substrate is added. Which reversible inhibition type is most consistent with this?

Show answer

Competitive inhibition.

21. What is a metabolic pathway?

Show answer

A series of reactions in which the product of one reaction becomes the substrate for the next.

22. What role does the product of one step usually play in the next step of a metabolic pathway?

Show answer

It acts as the substrate for the next reaction.

23. What is the end product of a metabolic pathway?

Show answer

The final product formed by the pathway.

24. What is end-product inhibition?

Show answer

Regulation in which an accumulated end product inhibits an enzyme earlier in its own metabolic pathway.

25. What triggers end-product inhibition?

Show answer

An increase in the end-product concentration.

26. Which enzyme is inhibited in the three-enzyme pathway example in the speech?

Show answer

Enzyme 1, an enzyme earlier in the pathway.

27. What happens to substrate 2 production when enzyme 1 is fully inhibited in the example?

Show answer

Substrate 2 is no longer produced by that reaction.

28. Why does inhibiting an early enzyme reduce later reactions in a pathway?

Show answer

It reduces the supply of intermediates needed as substrates for subsequent reactions.

29. How does end-product inhibition benefit a cell?

Show answer

It prevents excess product formation and conserves energy and resources.

30. Why is reversible inhibition important in end-product control?

Show answer

It allows the pathway to resume when the end-product concentration falls.

31. What is a cofactor?

Show answer

A non-protein substance required for, or assisting, an enzyme's activity.

32. How does a cofactor differ from an inhibitor in its effect on enzyme activity?

Show answer

A cofactor supports enzyme activity, whereas an inhibitor reduces it.

33. Which ion acts as a cofactor for amylase in the speech?

Show answer

Chloride ions.

34. What reaction does amylase help catalyse?

Show answer

The breakdown of starch into smaller sugars, such as maltose.

35. What is a coenzyme?

Show answer

An organic cofactor, often derived from a vitamin, that assists enzyme function.

36. How do coenzymes bind in the model described in the speech?

Show answer

They bind temporarily to enzymes.

37. Which vitamin is used to make coenzyme A?

Show answer

Vitamin B5.

38. What role does coenzyme A play in respiration?

Show answer

It carries acyl groups, including acetyl groups, helping link nutrient breakdown to aerobic respiration.

39. What is a prosthetic group?

Show answer

A cofactor that is tightly bound to an enzyme and remains attached during catalysis.

40. Name the ion and enzyme used as the prosthetic-group example, and state the enzyme's role.

Zinc ions in carbonic anhydrase; the enzyme catalyses the interconversion of carbon dioxide and water with bicarbonate and hydrogen ions, helping regulate blood pH.
