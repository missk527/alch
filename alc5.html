ALC5. The Mole and Molar Mass

Key Notes (Synced with Video)

What Is the Mole?
  • Atoms and particles are too small to count individually in ordinary laboratory samples.
  • The mole is a unit used to measure the amount of substance.
  • One mole contains the same number of particles as there are atoms in 12 g of carbon-12.
  • The symbol for the amount of substance is n, and its unit is the mole (mol).
Avogadro's Constant
  • One mole contains 6.02 × 1023 particles.
  • This value is called Avogadro's constant, NA.
  • The particles may be atoms, molecules, ions, or other specified entities.
  • For example, one mole of oxygen molecules contains 6.02 × 1023 oxygen molecules.
Molar Mass
  • Molar mass is the mass of one mole of a substance.
  • Its unit is grams per mole (g mol−1).
  • The numerical value of molar mass is the same as the relative atomic, molecular, or formula mass.
  • Different substances have different molar masses even though one mole of each contains the same number of particles.
Examples of Molar Mass
  • Carbon has a molar mass of 12 g mol−1.
  • Sulfur has a molar mass of approximately 32.1 g mol−1.
  • Copper has a molar mass of approximately 63.5 g mol−1.
  • Sodium has a relative atomic mass of 23, so its molar mass is 23 g mol−1.
The Mole Equation
  • Number of moles = mass ÷ molar mass.
  • In symbols: n = m ÷ M.
  • Rearrangements are m = nM and M = m ÷ n.
  • Use mass in grams and molar mass in g mol−1.
Worked Example: Barium
  • Question: How many moles are present in 0.22 g of barium?
  • The molar mass of barium is 137.33 g mol−1.
  • n = 0.22 ÷ 137.33 = 0.0016 mol = 1.6 × 10−3 mol.
Worked Example: Graphite
  • Graphite is a form of carbon, whose molar mass is 12 g mol−1.
  • For 0.642 g of graphite: n = 0.642 ÷ 12 = 0.0535 mol.
  • The number of atoms equals moles × Avogadro's constant.
  • Number of carbon atoms = 0.0535 × 6.02 × 1023 = 3.22 × 1022.
Exam Strategy and Summary
  • Identify the known quantities and the quantity required before selecting an equation.
  • Find atomic masses from the periodic table and add them when calculating a compound's molar mass.
  • Include units and express very large or small answers in scientific notation.
  • Use n = m/M to convert mass to moles, then multiply by 6.02 × 1023 to find the number of particles.
