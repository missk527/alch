ALC9. Titrations and Standard Solutions

Key Notes (Synced with Video)

Purpose of Titration
  • Titration is a form of volumetric analysis involving accurately measured solution volumes.
  • A known concentration is used to determine unknowns such as concentration, molar mass, formula, or water of crystallization.
  • A standard solution has an accurately known concentration.
The Titration Process
  • Use a pipette to transfer an accurate volume of solution X into a conical flask.
  • Add a suitable indicator, fill a burette with solution Y, and record the reading.
  • Add Y while swirling; near the end point, add it drop by drop.
  • Use the burette readings to determine the volume delivered at the end point.
Preparing a Standard Solution
  • Accurately weigh the solute, dissolve it in a beaker, and transfer it to a volumetric flask.
  • Rinse the beaker into the flask so that all solute is transferred quantitatively.
  • Add solvent to near the mark, then use a dropper until the bottom of the meniscus reaches the line.
  • Stopper and invert the flask several times to mix thoroughly. If the mark is exceeded, start again.
Equations and Units
  • Use n = cV, with concentration in mol dm⁻³ and volume in dm³.
  • Convert cm³ to dm³ by dividing by 1000.
  • Use m = nM and M = m/n for mass and molar-mass calculations.
  • Always use the balanced equation to apply the correct reacting mole ratio.
NaOH Standard Solution Example
  • 250 cm³ equals 0.250 dm³, so 0.100 mol dm⁻³ NaOH requires 0.0250 mol.
  • The molar mass of NaOH is 40.0 g mol⁻¹.
  • The required mass is 0.0250 × 40.0 = 1.00 g.
Unknown Concentration Example
  • HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O gives a 1:1 mole ratio.
  • 25.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ NaOH contains 0.00250 mol.
  • Therefore 22.5 cm³ of HCl contains 0.00250 mol, giving 0.111 mol dm⁻³.
Unknown Molar Mass Example
  • HX and NaOH react 1:1; 22.5 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ NaOH is 0.00225 mol.
  • A 25.0 cm³ HX aliquot therefore contains 0.00225 mol, while 250 cm³ contains 0.0225 mol.
  • For a 1.50 g sample, M = 1.50 ÷ 0.0225 = 66.7 g mol⁻¹.
Further Examples and Exam Technique
  • For 1.00 dm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ Mg(OH)₂, 5.83 g is required because M = 58.3 g mol⁻¹.
  • H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O gives a 1:2 acid-to-base ratio.
  • Ba(OH)₂ + 2HNO₃ → Ba(NO₃)₂ + 2H₂O gives a 1:2 base-to-acid ratio.
  • Use a weighing boat and volumetric flask; measure titration volumes with a pipette and burette; include units in every final answer.
