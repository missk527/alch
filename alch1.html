40C. Atomic Structure

Key Notes (Synced with Video)

0:00 - 0:40 | What Is an Atom?
  • An atom is the basic unit of matter and the smallest particle of a chemical element.
  • Almost everything around us is made of atoms, including people, food, planets, and galaxies.
  • Energy is not made of atoms, but matter is.
  • Atoms are too small to see directly, so scientists use models to explain their structure.
0:40 - 1:20 | Models of the Atom
  • Thomson's plum pudding model described the atom as a positive sphere with negative electrons inside it.
  • Rutherford's model introduced the idea of a small central nucleus.
  • Bohr's model showed electrons moving around the nucleus in shells.
  • Modern school-level diagrams often use a Bohr-style model to show atomic structure clearly.
1:20 - 2:10 | Atomic Structure
  • The nucleus is at the centre of the atom.
  • The nucleus is tiny and dense because it contains almost all of the atom's mass.
  • The nucleus contains protons and neutrons.
  • Protons and neutrons are called nucleons because they are found in the nucleus.
2:10 - 3:05 | Subatomic Particles
  • Electrons move around the nucleus in shells.
  • Electrons are extremely small and contribute very little to the mass of an atom.
  • Protons have a relative charge of +1.
  • Neutrons have no charge, and electrons have a relative charge of -1.
3:05 - 4:00 | Relative Mass and Neutral Atoms
  • Protons and neutrons each have a relative mass of 1.
  • Electrons have a relative mass of about 1/2000, so their mass is usually ignored in simple calculations.
  • In a neutral atom, the number of protons equals the number of electrons.
  • Equal positive and negative charges cancel out, so a neutral atom has no overall charge.
4:00 - 5:10 | Mass Number and Atomic Number
  • The atomic number is the number of protons in an atom.
  • The mass number is the total number of protons and neutrons.
  • The mass number is usually larger than the atomic number.
  • The number of neutrons can be calculated using: mass number - atomic number.
5:10 - 6:20 | Worked Examples
  • Aluminium has atomic number 13, so it has 13 protons.
  • A neutral aluminium atom has 13 electrons.
  • Aluminium with mass number 27 has 14 neutrons because 27 - 13 = 14.
  • Sodium with atomic number 11 has 11 electrons if it is neutral.
6:20 - End | Periodic Table and Exam Skills
  • The periodic table is arranged in order of increasing atomic number.
  • Vanadium comes before chromium because vanadium has atomic number 23 and chromium has atomic number 24.
  • For neutral atoms, protons and electrons are equal.
  • For questions about neutrons, subtract the atomic number from the mass number.
