Notes

  1. Atoms are the basic units of matter and the smallest particles of a chemical element.
  2. Everything around us, including people, food, planets, and galaxies, is made of atoms.
  3. Atomic models have changed over time, including Thomson’s plum pudding model, Rutherford’s nuclear model, and Bohr’s shell model.
  4. An atom has a tiny, dense nucleus at its centre, containing almost all of the atom’s mass.
  5. The nucleus contains protons and neutrons, which are collectively called nucleons.
  6. Electrons move around the nucleus in shells and have very little mass compared with protons and neutrons.
  7. Protons have a relative charge of +1 and a relative mass of 1.
  8. Neutrons have no charge and a relative mass of 1.
  9. Electrons have a relative charge of −1 and a very small relative mass, about 1/2000.
  10. In a neutral atom, the number of protons equals the number of electrons, so the overall charge is zero.
  11. The atomic number is the number of protons in an atom.
  12. The mass number is the total number of protons and neutrons in an atom.
  13. The number of neutrons can be calculated by subtracting the atomic number from the mass number.
  14. For aluminium, mass number 27 and atomic number 13 mean 13 protons, 13 electrons, and 14 neutrons.
  15. The periodic table is arranged in order of increasing atomic number, not mass number.
  16. Vanadium comes before chromium because vanadium has atomic number 23, while chromium has atomic number 24.
  17. For neutral atoms, electrons can be found from the atomic number because electrons equal protons.
  18. Exam questions often ask students to identify protons, neutrons, and electrons using mass number and atomic number.
