Notes

1. Carbon and covalent bonding

Organic chemistry studies carbon compounds. The simple organic molecules in this lesson contain atoms joined by covalent bonds; lines in displayed formulae represent these bonds.

  • Carbon normally forms four covalent bonds in these molecules.
  • Hydrogen forms one covalent bond.
  • Oxygen normally forms two covalent bonds.
  • Carbon atoms can bond together to form chains of different lengths.

2. Hydrocarbon chains and alcohol groups

Methanol, ethanol and propan-1-ol are examples of alcohols. Each contains a hydrocarbon part made of carbon and hydrogen, together with a hydroxyl group (–OH).

  • Methanol: CH3OH; one carbon atom.
  • Ethanol: CH3CH2OH; two carbon atoms.
  • Propan-1-ol: CH3CH2CH2OH; three carbon atoms.
  • The carbon chain becomes longer, but the –OH group stays the same.
  • In these alcohols, oxygen forms one bond to carbon and one bond to hydrogen.

3. Functional groups

A functional group is an atom or group of atoms in a molecule that determines its characteristic chemical reactions.

  • The functional group of an alcohol is the hydroxyl group, –OH.
  • Methanol, ethanol and propan-1-ol react in similar ways because they share this functional group.
  • Different functional groups give compounds different characteristic reactions.

4. Homologous series

A homologous series is a family of organic compounds with the same functional group and general formula, in which each successive member differs by CH2.

Successive member = one extra C + two extra H
  • Members have similar chemical properties because they have the same functional group.
  • Successive members differ by one CH2 unit, not just one carbon atom.
  • Members separated by two steps differ by C2H4.
  • Sharing a carbon-chain length alone does not mean that two compounds belong to the same series.

5. Alcohols as a homologous series

CH3OH → CH3CH2OH → CH3CH2CH2OH
  • Methanol to ethanol: add one carbon and two hydrogens.
  • Ethanol to propan-1-ol: add one carbon and two hydrogens.
  • All three molecules contain one oxygen atom and retain the –OH group.
  • For these saturated, open-chain alcohols with one –OH group, the general formula is CnH2n+1OH.
  • The next straight-chain primary alcohol is butan-1-ol, C4H9OH, with molecular formula C4H10O.

6. Chloroalkanes as another homologous series

In these chloroalkanes, a chlorine atom bonded to a saturated carbon forms the characteristic C–Cl functional group.

  • Chloromethane: CH3Cl.
  • Chloroethane: CH3CH2Cl.
  • 1-Chloropropane: CH3CH2CH2Cl.
  • Each successive member differs by CH2, while the C–Cl functional group remains.
  • For these saturated, open-chain compounds with one chlorine atom, the general formula is CnH2n+1Cl.

Alcohols and chloroalkanes are different homologous series because their functional groups differ. Chloroethane needs no position number because its two carbon positions are equivalent.
