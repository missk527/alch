ALC3. Relative Mass

Key Notes (Synced with Video)

Introduction | Why Relative Mass Is Used
  • Atoms are extremely small, with typical dimensions of about 10−8 cm.
  • Their actual masses are so tiny that relative values are more convenient than masses in kilograms.
  • Atomic masses are compared with the internationally agreed carbon-12 standard.
Carbon-12 and the Unified Atomic Mass Unit
  • A carbon-12 atom is assigned a mass of exactly 12 unified atomic mass units, written as 12 u.
  • One unified atomic mass unit is defined as one-twelfth of the mass of one carbon-12 atom.
  • One u is approximately 1.66054 × 10−27 kg.
Relative Isotopic Mass
  • Relative isotopic mass is the mass of an atom of an isotope compared with one-twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom.
  • It is approximately equal to the isotope’s mass number.
  • For example, chlorine-35 and chlorine-37 have relative isotopic masses of approximately 35 and 37.
Relative Atomic Mass (Ar)
  • Relative atomic mass is the weighted mean mass of an atom of an element compared with one-twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom.
  • It accounts for the relative abundances of all naturally occurring isotopes of an element.
  • Ar is usually not a whole number because most elements occur as mixtures of isotopes.
Calculating Relative Atomic Mass
  • Multiply each isotope’s relative isotopic mass by its percentage abundance.
  • Add the products and divide the total by 100.
  • For chlorine: [(35 × 75.77) + (37 × 24.23)] ÷ 100 ≈ 35.48.
  • For rubidium: [(85.00 × 72.15) + (87.00 × 27.85)] ÷ 100 = 85.557.
Relative Molecular Mass (Mr)
  • Relative molecular mass is the sum of the relative atomic masses of all atoms in one molecule.
  • It is used for substances made of discrete molecules, including diatomic elements such as O2, N2, and Cl2.
  • For Cl2: Mr = 2 × 35.45 = 70.9.
Relative Formula Mass
  • Relative formula mass is used for ionic compounds, which form giant lattices rather than discrete molecules.
  • It is calculated by adding the relative atomic masses of all atoms shown in one formula unit.
  • For NaCl: relative formula mass = 23.00 + 35.45 = 58.45.
Worked Example | Na3PO4
  • Na3PO4 contains three sodium atoms, one phosphorus atom, and four oxygen atoms.
  • Using Ar(Na) = 23, Ar(P) = 31, and Ar(O) = 16:
  • Relative formula mass = (3 × 23) + (1 × 31) + (4 × 16) = 69 + 31 + 64 = 164.
Exam Definitions and Common Misconceptions
  • A complete definition of Ar must include “weighted mean,” comparison with one-twelfth, and carbon-12.
  • The decimal value printed for an element in the periodic table is its relative atomic mass, not the mass number of one isotope.
  • For example, magnesium has Ar ≈ 24.31 because naturally occurring magnesium contains several isotopes.
  • Relative isotopic mass, relative atomic mass, relative molecular mass, and relative formula mass are ratios and therefore have no units.
Summary | Choosing the Correct Relative Mass
  • Use relative isotopic mass for one specific isotope.
  • Use Ar for an element, taking isotope abundances into account.
  • Use Mr for a discrete molecule.
  • Use relative formula mass for an ionic compound’s formula unit.
