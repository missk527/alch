ALC7. Atom Economy

Key Notes (Synced with Video)

Definition and Formula
  • Atom economy measures the mass of the desired product as a percentage of the mass of all products.
  • Atom economy = (molar mass of desired product ÷ total molar mass of all products) × 100.
  • Stoichiometric coefficients in the balanced equation must be included in the calculation.
  • The total mass of the products equals the total mass of the reactants because atoms are rearranged, not created or destroyed.
Dichloromethane Worked Example
  • Dichloromethane, CH₂Cl₂, is the desired product and HCl is the by-product.
  • The molar mass of CH₂Cl₂ is 85 g mol⁻¹, while the molar mass of HCl is 36.5 g mol⁻¹.
  • The reaction forms one mole of CH₂Cl₂ and two moles of HCl, giving a total product mass of 158 g per equation amount.
  • The atom economy is (85 ÷ 158) × 100 = 53.8%.
Importance of Atom Economy
  • Chemical reactions may produce unwanted by-products, which become chemical waste.
  • A higher atom economy reduces waste and uses raw materials more efficiently.
  • Improving atom economy makes chemical processes more sustainable.
  • This is especially important for reactions carried out on a large industrial scale.
Reaction Types
  • Addition reactions normally have 100% atom economy because all reactant atoms form one product.
  • Addition across a double bond produces no by-products.
  • Substitution and elimination reactions usually have atom economies below 100%.
  • These reactions form more than one product, so unwanted by-products may be produced.
Ethanol Production
  • Fermentation converts glucose into two moles of ethanol and two moles of carbon dioxide.
  • Ethanol has a molar mass of 46 g mol⁻¹ and carbon dioxide has a molar mass of 44 g mol⁻¹.
  • The desired ethanol mass is 92 g and the total product mass is 180 g, giving an atom economy of 51.1%.
  • Hydration of ethene produces only ethanol, so its atom economy is 100%.
  • Hydration is preferable by atom economy because it produces no by-products.
Magnesium Hydroxide Example
  • The balanced equation is MgCl₂ + 2NaOH → Mg(OH)₂ + 2NaCl.
  • Magnesium hydroxide is the desired product; sodium chloride is the by-product.
  • The molar masses are 58.3 g mol⁻¹ for Mg(OH)₂ and 58.5 g mol⁻¹ for NaCl.
  • The atom economy is 58.3 ÷ [58.3 + (2 × 58.5)] × 100 = 33.3%.
Theoretical Mass and Percentage Yield
  • Moles are calculated using moles = mass ÷ molar mass.
  • Ten grams of MgCl₂ corresponds to approximately 0.105 mol.
  • The 1:1 ratio between MgCl₂ and Mg(OH)₂ gives approximately 0.105 mol of Mg(OH)₂, with a maximum mass of about 6.1 g.
  • Percentage yield = (actual yield ÷ theoretical yield) × 100.
  • An actual mass of 5.0 g from a theoretical 6.1 g gives a yield of 82.0%.
Atom Economy Compared with Yield
  • Atom economy and percentage yield measure different aspects of a reaction.
  • Atom economy is determined by the balanced equation and the proportion of desired products.
  • Percentage yield compares the amount actually obtained with the theoretical maximum.
  • A reaction can have a low atom economy but a high percentage yield, as in the magnesium hydroxide example.
