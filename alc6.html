84B. Percentage Yield and Limiting Reagents

Key Notes (Synced with Video)

Percentage Yield | Meaning and Formula
  • Percentage yield measures the effectiveness of a synthetic procedure.
  • Percentage yield = (actual yield / theoretical yield) × 100%.
  • It shows what percentage of the reactants has been successfully converted into the desired product.
Why Yield Is Often Below 100%
  • Side reactions may produce unwanted by-products.
  • Some reactants may remain unreacted, especially when a reaction does not go to completion or reaches equilibrium.
  • Some product may be lost during separation, purification, or transfer.
Limiting Reagents
  • The limiting reagent is used up first and determines the maximum amount of product that can form.
  • An excess reagent is present in more than the amount required and remains after the limiting reagent is consumed.
  • To identify the limiting reagent, convert each reactant mass to moles and compare the available amounts using the balanced-equation ratio.
Cake Analogy
  • If one cake needs 3 eggs and 100 g of flour, 10 eggs can make only 3 complete cakes, while 1 kg of flour can make 10.
  • Eggs are therefore limiting and flour is in excess; the limiting ingredient controls the number of cakes.
Core Mole Calculations
  • Number of moles = mass / molar mass.
  • Mass = number of moles × molar mass.
  • Always use the coefficients in the balanced equation to convert between moles of reactant and moles of product.
Nitrogen and Hydrogen Example
  • For N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃, 0.5 g N₂ is about 0.0179 mol and 0.6 g H₂ is 0.300 mol.
  • The reaction needs 3 mol H₂ per 1 mol N₂; the available hydrogen is more than sufficient, so nitrogen is limiting.
Ethane Combustion Example
  • For 2C₂H₆ + 7O₂ → 4CO₂ + 6H₂O, 16 g of ethane is 0.533 mol.
  • The 1:2 ethane-to-carbon-dioxide ratio gives about 1.067 mol CO₂, or a theoretical mass of about 46.9 g.
  • An actual CO₂ yield of 22 g gives a percentage yield of about 46.9%.
Ammonia Example
  • For N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃, excess nitrogen means hydrogen is the limiting reagent.
  • Ten moles of H₂ theoretically produce 6.667 mol NH₃, equivalent to about 113.33 g.
  • An actual ammonia yield of 20.25 g gives a percentage yield of about 17.9%.
Water Example
  • For 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, 3.5 g H₂ is 1.75 mol and 30 g O₂ is 0.9375 mol.
  • The equation requires 2 mol H₂ for each 1 mol O₂, so hydrogen is the limiting reagent.
  • Hydrogen theoretically produces 1.75 mol, or 31.5 g, of water; an actual yield of 29 g is about 92.1%.
Multi-step Aspirin Example
  • For consecutive steps, multiply decimal yields: 0.90 × 0.80 × 0.98 = 0.7056, so the overall yield is 70.56%.
  • One tonne of sodium phenoxide is 1,000,000 g, or about 8,621 mol at 116 g mol⁻¹.
  • With a 1:1 mole ratio and a 70.56% overall yield, about 6,083 mol of aspirin forms.
  • At 180 g mol⁻¹, the aspirin mass is about 1.095 tonnes.
Exam Strategy
  • Write the balanced equation, identify the actual yield, and calculate the theoretical yield from the limiting reagent.
  • Keep units consistent, show mole ratios clearly, and convert percentages to decimals when multiplying step yields.
  • Unfamiliar chemical names do not change the method: use the supplied masses, molar masses, ratios, and yields.
