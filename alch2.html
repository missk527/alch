AL Chemistry 2. Atomic Structure

Key Notes (Synced with Video)

0:00 - 0:28 | Introduction to Atoms
  • Atoms are the basic units of matter and the smallest particles of chemical elements.
  • Nearly everything around us is made of atoms, including people, food, planets, and galaxies.
  • The lesson introduces atomic structure, subatomic particles, mass number, atomic number, and examples from the periodic table.
0:28 - 1:10 | Models of the Atom
  • Several atomic models have been suggested over time.
  • Thomson's plum pudding model described a positive sphere with negative electrons embedded in it.
  • Rutherford's model introduced the idea of a central nucleus.
  • Bohr's model showed electrons arranged in shells around the nucleus.
1:10 - 2:10 | Atomic Structure
  • The nucleus is tiny, dense, and located at the centre of the atom.
  • Almost all of an atom's mass is found in the nucleus.
  • The nucleus contains protons and neutrons, which are collectively called nucleons.
  • Electrons move around the nucleus in shells and contribute very little to the atom's mass.
2:10 - 3:24 | Subatomic Particles
  • Atoms contain three main subatomic particles: protons, neutrons, and electrons.
  • Protons have a positive charge, neutrons have no charge, and electrons have a negative charge.
  • Protons and neutrons each have a relative mass of 1.0.
  • Electrons are much smaller, with a relative mass of about 1/2000.
3:24 - 4:08 | Neutral Atoms
  • In a neutral atom, the number of protons equals the number of electrons.
  • The positive charge from protons balances the negative charge from electrons.
  • A neutral atom has no overall charge and is not an ion.
4:08 - 5:24 | Mass Number and Atomic Number
  • Element symbols in the periodic table can show mass number and atomic number.
  • The mass number is the total number of protons and neutrons.
  • The atomic number is the number of protons.
  • The mass number is usually larger than the atomic number because it includes both protons and neutrons.
5:24 - 6:40 | Aluminium Example
  • Aluminium has a mass number of 27 and an atomic number of 13.
  • The atomic number tells us aluminium has 13 protons.
  • A neutral aluminium atom also has 13 electrons.
  • The number of neutrons is found by subtracting the atomic number from the mass number: 27 - 13 = 14.
6:40 - 7:35 | Sodium Exam Example
  • Sodium has a mass number of 23 and an atomic number of 11.
  • The atomic number tells us sodium has 11 protons.
  • Because the atom is neutral, sodium also has 11 electrons.
7:35 - 8:35 | Periodic Table Order
  • Elements are arranged in the periodic table by atomic number, not mass number.
  • Vanadium comes before chromium because vanadium has atomic number 23 and chromium has atomic number 24.
  • An exam answer should mention the periodic table order and the two atomic numbers.
8:35 - 10:10 | Calculating Subatomic Particles
  • Calcium has atomic number 20, so it has 20 protons and 20 electrons in a neutral atom.
  • Calcium has mass number 40, so it has 40 - 20 = 20 neutrons.
  • Platinum has atomic number 78, so it has 78 protons and 78 electrons in a neutral atom.
  • Platinum has mass number 195, so it has 195 - 78 = 117 neutrons.
